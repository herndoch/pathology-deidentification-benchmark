# Preserving Molecular Information During Pathology Report De-identification: A Synthetic Benchmark

**Background:** Pathology reports can support model-assisted standardization, summarization, hypothesis generation and testing, and research abstraction. De-identification must remove identifiers without erasing molecular findings.

**Design:** We compared an institution-developed pipeline, context-aware rules, and their frozen refinement with five frontier LLM configurations (GPT-5.6 Sol through two interfaces, Claude Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4.6) on 200 unique synthetic report pairs. Joint success required preserving a molecular expression in one report and removing similarly formatted identifying digits in its paired control. A separate 200-pair experiment examined eight local failure patterns. Exploratory comparisons used paired tests and analyses grouping shared report patterns, with adjustment for multiple comparisons.

**Results:** Joint success was 0/200 for the institution-developed pipeline, 133/200 for context-aware rules, 156/200 for refined rules, and 197-200/200 for LLM configurations. The pipeline retained no complete molecular expressions; both local rule methods retained 159/200, versus 200/200 for every LLM configuration. Refined rules left identifying digits visible in 3/200 controls, versus 0-3/200 for LLM configurations. LLM-versus-rule differences had paired adjusted p<0.001; pattern-grouped analyses were inconclusive (adjusted p=.156 versus earlier rules; .375 versus refined rules). Local failures included molecular over-redaction and identifiers missed across line breaks, separated footers, parentheses, and modified labels. In the separate experiment, refinement halved exposures from 100/200 to 50/200, with parenthetical and modified-label failures persisting.

**Conclusions:** Local refinement reduced identifier exposure but left substantial molecular information loss. LLM configurations achieved both objectives more consistently in this synthetic benchmark; safety and performance on independent clinical reports remain unestablished.
